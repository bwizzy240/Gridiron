Model blends scoring differential (Pythagorean win expectation), an opponent-adjusted power rating (SRS, computed iteratively from the schedule so beating a good team counts for more than beating a bad one), season record, home/away-specific record, and recent form, adds a small home-field edge and a preseason strength prior (from Kalshi's season win-total market, fading out as real games accumulate), then applies a calibration curve fit from a 1,360-game, 5-season backtest, before layering on rest since last game, travel distance/time-zone shift, weather, and starting-QB status — it is not a guarantee and doesn't account for everything a sportsbook prices in. The spread market accounts for NFL "key numbers" (final margins cluster heavily at 3 and 7 because of how scoring works) instead of assuming a smooth bell curve, using published historical frequencies rather than a plain normal distribution; totals don't get this treatment, since reliable key-number data for combined scores isn't as well documented. Schedule, scores and standings are pulled live from ESPN's public scoreboard where available; weather comes from Open-Meteo; moneyline odds and the preseason prior come from Kalshi's public market data via a same-origin proxy; spread and total odds need a free Odds API key (see button above); the starting QB comes from ESPN's actual depth chart (ranked, not just whoever played most recently) along with that player's own current status; the injury report loads live from ESPN when you expand a game's details panel. Any of these can silently fall back to a generated sample or a neutral default if the live source doesn't respond — nothing here is guaranteed. Use this as one input, not the whole decision.
